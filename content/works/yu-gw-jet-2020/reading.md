# Gravitational-wave memory from a propagating relativistic jet: a probe of the interior of gamma-ray burst progenitors

## Why This Work Matters

A pioneering theoretical paper connecting relativistic jet propagation with gravitational-wave physics. It proposes that the directional mass and energy redistribution as a jet burrows through stellar envelopes produces measurable non-oscillatory GW memory.

## Problem

When a newly launched relativistic jet is choked inside an optically thick stellar envelope or ejecta, how can gravitational waves probe its hidden deceleration, collimation, and breakout?

## Scientific takeaway

Derives the linear and non-linear gravitational-wave memory strain generated during asymmetric jet propagation, obtaining characteristic amplitudes $$h\sim10^{-26}-10^{-23}$$ in the deci-Hz to Hz band directly probing jet momentum dissipation and engine duration.

## Assumptions

- Jet head experiences strong momentum dissipation into cocoon layers, altering mass quadrupole moments.
- Highly collimated, anisotropic relativistic momentum transport modeled in linearized gravity.
- Considers detector noise curves of next-generation decihertz and ground-based interferometers.

## Scientific delta

Pioneers the use of non-oscillatory gravitational wave memory as a non-electromagnetic probe of choked and burrowing jets, expanding GW astrophysics beyond standard inspiral-merger chirps.

## Reason to read

Prime example of cross-messenger theory. Focus on the integration of the source stress-energy tensor yielding non-vanishing net transverse strain shifts in the far field.
