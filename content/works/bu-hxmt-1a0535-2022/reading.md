# High energy millihertz quasi-periodic oscillations in 1A 0535 + 262 with Insight-HXMT challenge current models

## Why This Work Matters

A flagship observational discovery for the R7 wing using China's Insight-HXMT observatory. It detects millihertz quasi-periodic oscillations (mHz QPOs) in the accreting pulsar 1A 0535+262 above 100 keV, directly challenging standard Keplerian disk truncation models.

## Problem

Do millihertz QPOs in accreting neutron star binaries originate from Keplerian orbital blobs at the inner disk edge or from magnetospheric accretion column instabilities extending to hard X-rays?

## Scientific takeaway

Finds significant mHz QPO signals extending up to 100 keV, proving the oscillation zone resides deep within the magnetospheric accretion column. Using the beat-frequency framework $$ \nu_{\rm QPO} \approx \nu_{\rm K}(r_0) - \nu_s $$, it confirms magnetic polar-cap shock modulation, providing tight constraints on accretion flow geometry.

## Assumptions

- Accretion flow is captured from the companion Be disk and channeled along dipole field lines.
- Hard X-rays originate in radiative shock settling columns above the neutron star polar caps.
- Timing power spectra can be decomposed into Lorentzian components with energy-dependent phase lags.

## Scientific delta

Pushes the detection limit of accreting pulsar QPOs beyond 100 keV for the first time, overthrowing thermal disk-origin assumptions and directly bridging engine accretion (R1) with high-energy timing (R7).
