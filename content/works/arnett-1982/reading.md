# Type I supernovae: early light-curve solutions

## Scientific takeaway

Arnett develops an analytic account of early Type I supernova light curves in
which radioactive heating, expansion, and radiative diffusion jointly shape
the observable optical evolution.

## Problem

How can a compact physical model connect a radioactive energy source and
expanding ejecta to the evolving optical light curve, effective temperature,
and broad-band colors?

## Assumptions

- The ejecta undergo homologous expansion.
- Radioactive nickel is present in the ejected matter.
- The blackbody supernova construct is used to represent the escaping thermal
  spectrum.

## Scientific delta

This Work supplies an analytic light-curve, effective-temperature, and
broad-band color solution that makes the radioactive-heating and diffusion
balance explicit. It is a Work-level physical account, not a modern subtype
reclassification of the historical “Type I” label.

## Reason to read

Read this Work to see how a compact physical model connects an energy source to
transport and a measurable light curve. Its historical “Type I” language should
not be silently rewritten as a modern subtype classification.
