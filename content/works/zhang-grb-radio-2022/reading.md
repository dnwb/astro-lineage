# Diagnosing Circumburst Environment with Multiband Gamma-Ray Burst Radio Afterglows

## Why This Work Matters

A benchmark methodology paper for diagnosing circumstellar environments via multi-band radio afterglows. It develops exact inverse-mapping relations for arbitrary power-law ambient media using radio peak times and fluxes.

## Problem

Given that optical and X-ray afterglows suffer severe degeneracies with cooling and injection, how can multi-band radio peaks uniquely constrain the power-law density index of the circumstellar environment?

## Scientific takeaway

Adopts a generalized power-law ambient profile $$n=A_{\ast}R^{-k}$$ and establishes analytic equations relating multi-band radio peak times and fluxes to $k$. Sample analysis reveals that real environments frequently deviate from pure ISM ($k=0$) or stellar winds ($k=2$), exhibiting intermediate profile gradients.

## Assumptions

- Circumstellar density obeys a monotonic power-law $n \propto R^{-k}$ over the deceleration radius range.
- Radio emission is dominated by relativistic forward shock synchrotron and self-absorption processes.
- Microphysical equipartition parameters remain steady over the radio observation baseline.

## Scientific delta

Transmutes the crude dichotomy of 'ISM vs steady wind' into a continuum estimation of the density slope $k$, establishing a multi-band radio inversion diagnostic toolkit.

## Reason to read

Methodological guide for radio afterglow modeling and progenitor mass-loss diagnostics. Focus on the equations solving $k$ directly from self-absorption and peak-flux trajectories.
