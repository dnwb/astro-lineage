# The Landscape of Thermal Transients from Supernova Interacting with a Circumstellar Medium

## Why This Work Matters

An advanced framework unifying ejecta-CSM shock dynamics with radiative diffusion. It establishes universal physical scaling relations connecting peak optical luminosity, rise time, and shock breakout morphology to dense CSM configurations.

## Problem

When circumstellar material forms a dense confined shell or steady wind, is the transient light curve peak dictated by shock breakout or delayed radiative diffusion, and how can they be distinguished?

## Scientific takeaway

Derives analytical relations for peak luminosity and peak time $$t_{\rm peak}\sim\frac{\kappa M_{\rm ej}}{4\pi c R}$$, demonstrating how optical depth transitions across the shock front govern the diversity between fast shock breakouts and broad diffusion peaks.

## Assumptions

- Spherical symmetry under local thermodynamic equilibrium and grey-opacity diffusion approximations.
- Dense CSM opacity is sufficient to thermalize and trap kinetic shock dissipation in early phases.
- Neglects 3D Rayleigh-Taylor Rayleigh mixing channels and multidimensional funnel leakage.

## Scientific delta

Extends beyond Arnett radioactive diffusion formulas to establish a self-consistent analytic framework for shock-heated diffusion, linking Chevalier self-similarity to TransFit numerical solvers.

## Reason to read

Crucial for analyzing optical rise times of CSM-interacting transients. Study the dimensionless transition parameter $\xi$ that delineates shock breakout from delayed diffusion peaks.
