# Neutron Star Mergers in AGN Accretion Disks: Cocoon and Ejecta Shock Breakouts

## Why This Work Matters

A foundational pioneering paper establishing dense-environment multi-messenger astrophysics (R6). It predicts that double neutron star mergers embedded in AGN accretion disk migration traps produce distinctive dual shock breakout signatures that redefine kilonova searches.

## Problem

While merger counterparts are canonically sought in low-density galactic fields, how are relativistic jets, cocoons, and ejecta modified when mergers occur within the ultra-dense gaseous midplanes of AGN disks?

## Scientific takeaway

Demonstrates that merger jets are choked within the dense disk, inflating superheated cocoons that break out at $\sim 0.15$ days with an extreme X-ray flash reaching $$L_{\rm pk}\sim10^{46}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}$$, followed by a slower sub-relativistic ejecta UV breakout at $\sim 0.5$ days, creating a unique multi-messenger signature.

## Assumptions

- Binary neutron stars migrate into AGN disk traps and merge inside dense gas-dominated midplanes.
- AGN disk midplane maintains extreme densities ($10^{-10}-10^{-9}\text{ g cm}^{-3}$) and scale heights.
- Merger energetics and ejecta masses follow canonical double neutron star parameters ($10^{50}-10^{51}\text{ erg}$).

## Scientific delta

Overturns the assumption that kilonovae are universally faint optical/infrared transients, pioneering the AGN-disk environmental paradigm and steering searches toward intense early X-ray and UV shock breakouts.

## Reason to read

Essential reading for dense-environment multi-messenger transients. Focus on the first-principles equations governing cocoon thermal energy accumulation and disk-breakout timescales.
