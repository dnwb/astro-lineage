# γ-Ray Bursts and Afterglows from Rotating Strange Stars and Neutron Stars

## Why This Work Matters

The groundbreaking seminal paper pioneering the millisecond magnetar central engine model for GRBs and afterglows. It proposed that continuous magnetic dipole energy injection fundamentally modifies shock dynamics, predicting post-burst afterglow plateaus.

## Problem

Classical fireball models assumed impulsive, instantaneous energy release, failing to explain flattening afterglow plateaus and extreme blast energetics. Can a long-lived central compact remnant provide continuous energy injection?

## Scientific takeaway

Derives the continuous magnetic dipole power injection formulation $$ L_{\rm dip}(t) = L_0 \left(1 + \frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{-2} $$. Proves that continuous energy replenishment arrests shock deceleration, producing an extended afterglow plateau and establishing the theoretical foundation for magnetar-driven transients.

## Assumptions

- The central engine forms a long-lived, rapidly rotating, highly magnetized neutron star or strange quark star.
- Rotational braking is dominated by magnetic dipole radiation ($n=3$) channeling Poynting flux outward.
- Injected Poynting flux catches up with the decelerating blast wave and thermalizes into downstream shock energy.

## Scientific delta

Shatters the impulsive fireball doctrine by inventing the long-lived millisecond magnetar engine paradigm, directly inspiring Kasen & Bildsten 2010, Yu et al. 2013, and the broader engine-powered transient discipline.

## Reason to read

The historical and conceptual wellspring for all magnetar energy-injection theory. Trace the modified blast-wave deceleration $\Gamma(t) \propto t^{-(1-q)/8}$ driven by continuous dipole power.
