# Long-term evolution of cyclotron resonant scattering features in the accreting pulsar Vela X-1: A pulse-to-pulse approach

## Why This Work Matters

A gold-standard study for measuring neutron star surface magnetic fields directly from X-ray observations. Utilizing decades of satellite data and pulse-to-pulse spectroscopy, it tracks the cyclotron resonant scattering feature (CRSF) evolution in Vela X-1.

## Problem

How does the cyclotron line energy shift with accretion luminosity, where is the critical transition luminosity dividing the sub-critical and super-critical regimes, and is the surface magnetic field structurally stable over decades?

## Scientific takeaway

Applies the cyclotron resonant scattering relation $$ E_{\rm CRSF} \approx 11.6\,{\rm keV}\,\left(\frac{B}{10^{12}\,{\rm G}}\right)(1+z)^{-1} $$ to measure a surface polar field of $B \sim 2.6\times 10^{12}\text{ G}$, demonstrating that line shifts reflect dynamic variations in accretion column shock heights with instantaneous luminosity.

## Assumptions

- Resonant absorption lines arise from electron Landau level transitions in quantized magnetic fields.
- Gravitational redshift is fixed by canonical neutron star compactness ($1+z \sim 1.3$).
- Cross-instrument responses among NuSTAR, HXMT, and Suzaku are cross-calibrated self-consistently.

## Scientific delta

Pioneers pulse-to-pulse phase-resolved spectroscopy to eliminate time-averaged spectral blurring, providing the most robust empirical magnetic benchmark linking magnetic theory (R5) to observation (R7).

## Reason to read

Indispensable for mastering cyclotron line physics and X-ray spectroscopy. Focus on the joint fitting techniques decoupling the continuum (NPEX/cutoffpl) from cyclotron absorption profiles (Gabs).
