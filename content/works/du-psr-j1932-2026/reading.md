# Radio eclipse of the slowest spinning Galactic-field spider pulsar PSR J1932+2121 and its X-ray emission prospect

## Why This Work Matters

A cutting-edge observational and theoretical investigation using FAST radio observations to bridge compact binary environments and high-energy shock physics. It exploits the slowest known Galactic field redback pulsar to probe intrabinary shock geometry.

## Problem

With an unusually wide 1.9-day orbit, how can a low-mass companion wind maintain dense enough material to produce severe broadband radio eclipses, and does the intrabinary shock still power detectable X-rays?

## Scientific takeaway

Measures precise pulse-phase-resolved eclipse flux profiles and DM variations with FAST. Synchrotron self-absorption modeling yields a nearly edge-on inclination $$i_{\rm o}\simeq 88.55^\circ$$ with modest companion mass-loss, predicting Doppler-boosted double-peaked X-ray emission from the intrabinary shock.

## Assumptions

- Radio eclipse mechanism is governed by synchrotron self-absorption in the shocked companion wind plasma.
- Excess dispersion $\Delta\text{DM}$ accurately tracks the integrated line-of-sight free electron column density.
- Assumes stable pulsar wind injection in an unperturbed Keplerian binary orbit.

## Scientific delta

Achieves a closed causal inversion from low-frequency radio propagation effects to high-energy shock X-ray predictions, providing a standardized template for FAST-discovered spider systems.

## Reason to read

Prime paper demonstrating how FAST radio timing and eclipse data inform high-energy astrophysics. Study the numerical optical depth integrations constraining the $i_{\rm o} \simeq 88.55^\circ$ inclination.
