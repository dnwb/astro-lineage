# Spectra and Light Curves of Gamma-Ray Burst Afterglows

## Why This Work Matters

The definitive paper establishing the standard analytic model for GRB afterglows. By coupling BM76 blast-wave deceleration with synchrotron emission from shock-accelerated power-law electrons, it provides piecewise power-law spectra and light curves across radio to X-rays.

## Problem

How can broadband afterglow light curves and spectral breaks be analytically predicted from microphysical shock parameters and electron cooling in a decelerating relativistic blast wave?

## Scientific takeaway

Derives piecewise synchrotron spectra separated by the injection frequency $\nu_m$ and cooling frequency $\nu_c$. Establishes closure relations $$F_\nu \propto t^{-\alpha}\nu^{-\beta}$$ for fast and slow cooling regimes, along with characteristic scalings for synchrotron self-absorption $\nu_a$.

## Assumptions

- Assumes constant microphysical shock fractions ($epsilon_B$, $epsilon_e$) and electron spectral index $p$ over time.
- Spherical adiabatic BM76 shock dynamics expanding into a constant-density ISM.
- Pure synchrotron emission from isotropic electrons, neglecting inverse Compton feedback on cooling.

## Scientific delta

Bridges relativistic fluid dynamics to directly observable multi-band power-law flux scalings, establishing closure relations between temporal slope $\alpha$ and spectral index $\beta$.

## Reason to read

Core reference for afterglow modeling and environmental diagnostics. Focus on the derivation of slow-cooling spectral breaks and standard closure relations in the $\nu_m < \nu < \nu_c$ regime.
